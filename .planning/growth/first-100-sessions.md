# First 100 paid sessions: Irvine + LA

Working doc. Open this on your phone at the gym.

**Goal:** 100 completed, paid sessions in Irvine and LA.
**Hard deadline driving it:** Founding Trainer 0% fee closes for new signups **2026-10-01**.
**Scoreboard:** count of `bookings` with status `completed`. Nothing else counts.

---

## Why this works without a big audience

15 activated trainers x 3 of their own existing clients x 2 sessions each = 90 sessions. That happens before a single stranger watches a video.

Trainers already have paying clients. You are not asking them to find new ones. You are giving them a reason to move the ones they have, and the reason is that they keep 100% for twelve months if they sign before October.

The show is the second engine. It is what makes trainer 26 through 100 come to you instead of you chasing them, and it gives every trainer something they actually want to post.

One thing to keep straight: Founding Trainer sessions earn FitRush 0% for a year. Stripe still takes its cut. The first 100 sessions prove the thing works, they do not make money. That is the right trade in August. It should not be a surprise in October.

## Part 1: get 25 trainers

### Who to target

Independent and contract PTs. Not salaried big-box staff who are contractually barred from outside clients.

**Irvine and OC:** LA Fitness and 24 Hour in Irvine and Costa Mesa, CrossFit boxes across the county, boutique and private studios in Newport, Tustin, Lake Forest. Boutique coaches want side clients and are the easiest yes in the whole list.

**LA:** independents at Gold's and Powerhouse, private studios in Culver City, Sherman Oaks, DTLA, Silver Lake.

### Building the list without leaving the couch

Instagram location tags on each gym above, plus city hashtags. Every PT in Orange County has a public IG with "DM to train" in the bio. That is the list. Two hundred names in a week.

### The DM

Short. Leads with money. Has a deadline.

> Hey, I'm Xavier, I run FitRush. You keep 100% of what you charge for your first 12 months if you're in before Oct 1, after that it's 13%. No monthly fee. I'm also filming a series where I train with local PTs and we just talk shop, and I'd put you in it. Takes about 10 minutes to set up your profile. Want the link?

### If they say "what's the catch"

> No catch and no contract. We take 13% after your first year, that's it. You set your own rate and your own hours. If it doesn't bring you clients you just stop using it.

### If they say "I already have enough clients"

> Then this is easier. Put the clients you already train on it, you keep 100% for a year, and you get $10 every time one of them refers someone. It's not asking you to find anybody new.

### Sign 5 to 8 in person

Walk them through onboarding on their phone at the gym. It takes under ten minutes and they show up in search before you leave. That moment sells the product better than anything you can say about it.

### The activation message, day one of every account

A signed-up trainer with no clients is worth nothing. Send this the day they finish onboarding.

> Two things. Your referral link is in your dashboard. Send it to three clients you already train. When they finish their first session you get $10, they get $5 off, and you keep 100% of the session. Second, when do you want to film?

That message is the actual growth engine. It turns supply into demand in one move.

### Ask every single trainer this

> Who else at your gym should be on this?

PTs at the same gym are in group chats. One trainer who likes you brings five. This compounds faster than posting does.

### Tracker

A spreadsheet. Twenty-five rows. Do not build anything for this.

`name | city | gym | IG | DM sent | signed up | profile complete | filmed | clients invited | sessions completed`

## Part 2: Train Therapy

### What it is

You training with a real FitRush PT, asking the questions their clients are too polite to ask, while you are too winded to sound scripted.

The workout is the container, not the content. Nobody watches a stranger squat. They watch a person be funny and specific about something they know cold.

**The one rule:** every episode has to make a viewer think "I would pay that person." A fun episode where the trainer never demonstrates competence is entertainment. It books nothing.

### Gear and format

Full cut under six minutes. Three to five verticals at 20 to 45 seconds. Phone on a tripod or cheap gimbal, two clip-on wireless mics, natural gym light.

No logo intro. No music under the talking. Gym ambience reads as real, a music bed reads as an ad.

Shoot two episodes per gym visit. You are already there, already miked, already sweaty.

### Beat sheet

**0:00 to 0:03, cold open.** No intro, no logo, no "welcome back." Open on the trainer's single most opinionated sentence of the entire shoot, mid-set. You will find it in the edit, so film everything, including setup and walking around.

**0:03 to 0:08, place the person.** "This is Marcus. He trains out of a garage gym in Irvine and he's about to tell me my deadlift is a moral failure." Name, city, joke. The city is not optional. It is the whole point of the platform.

**Body, three question blocks.** Between sets, never during. Pull from the bank. Stay on a thread if it is good, bail if it is not. You are not getting through a list.

**Fix Me. This is the segment that converts.** You do a lift badly, on purpose or genuinely. The trainer changes one thing. You do it again and the difference is visible on camera. Nothing you say sells a trainer as well as a viewer watching your bar path straighten out in eight seconds. Every episode. No exceptions.

**Rapid fire while gassed.** Immediately after a hard set, when you are both breathing too hard to be careful. This is where the funny lives.

**Close, the booking bridge.** Always last, always the same question: "Somebody in Irvine books you tomorrow. What does session one actually look like?" That answer is the sales pitch in their own voice and it tells the viewer exactly what they would be buying. Then say it plain: "Marcus is on FitRush. Link's in the bio, he's in Irvine, go book him."

### Question bank

**Where everyone gets it wrong**
- What's the most useless machine in this gym? Point at it.
- What do people ask you for that they don't actually need?
- Someone says they want abs in six weeks. What do you say to their face?
- I'm 40, desk job, bad lower back, three hours a week. What are my first 30 days?
- Which supplement is a scam?
- Rank these for a beginner: sleep, protein, the program, showing up. Defend it.
- What's a cue everyone repeats that's wrong?
- Why is my chest not growing? Be mean about it.
- What would you cut from every beginner's program?
- What's the one thing you'd make illegal in gyms?

**Confession**
- Last time you skipped a workout, and what was the excuse?
- What's your worst lift and how bad is it really?
- What did you believe five years ago that you'd argue with yourself about now?
- What do you eat when nobody's filming?
- Ever hurt yourself doing something dumb in front of a client?
- What part of your own training do you avoid?

**Clients, no names**
- Weirdest thing a client has said to you mid-set?
- Who's the client you're proudest of, and what actually changed for them?
- What kind of client do you turn down?
- What's the first thing you notice about someone in the first five minutes?
- What do people lie to you about, and do you call it out?
- Somebody ghosts after two sessions. Do you text them?

**Hot takes, this is what travels**
- Cardio before or after lifting, and how much do you actually care?
- Is soreness a signal or a vibe?
- One popular exercise you'd delete from the internet?
- Machines or free weights, and is that even a real fight?
- Is a trainer worth it for someone who already knows how to lift?
- Dumbest fitness trend you've watched come back?

**Rapid fire, film this while you're both wrecked**
- Coffee or pre-workout
- Squat or deadlift
- Morning or night
- Rep range you hate
- Cheat meal
- One exercise for the rest of your life
- Worst gym music genre
- Rest day: real rest or "active recovery"

**The bridge, ask every time**
- Somebody in [city] books you tomorrow. What does session one look like?
- Who is your ideal client? Describe them like you're picking them out of a crowd.
- What should someone bring to their first session with you?

Use the "ideal client" answer as the caption. It is free targeting, written by the trainer.

### Shot list per episode

1. Wide establishing shot of the gym, five seconds, bed for the cold open
2. Trainer talking mid-set, handheld, close
3. Fix Me: before angle, correction, after angle. Same framing both times or the difference does not read
4. Rapid fire, both of you in frame, breathing hard
5. Closing bridge, trainer alone, looking at camera
6. B-roll of the trainer coaching, no audio needed

### Caption template

> [Trainer name] trains out of [gym], [city].
>
> [Their "ideal client" answer, one or two sentences, their words.]
>
> He's on FitRush. Link in bio.

## Part 3: distribution, and the part most people skip

Per episode: one long cut, three to five verticals each built on one hot take or the Fix Me moment, one carousel of five slides of the trainer's actual advice with the booking link on the last slide.

Then the mechanic that matters more than all of it: **the trainer reposts everything.**

Every trainer has a few hundred to a few thousand local followers who already like them and already live nearby. Twenty-five trainers reposting reaches more of the right people than @fitrush.io does, and it is warm traffic instead of cold. You are not building an audience. You are borrowing twenty-five of them.

Make reposting part of the deal, stated in the DM before you ever film. I put you in the show, you post it, you keep 100%, and you get $10 every time somebody books through your link.

Their link should point at their own FitRush profile with their referral code attached, so the click lands on their face and the payout still fires.

### Other demand, all supplementary

- Reply to every "where is he located" comment publicly with the city and the link. That is a lead answering itself.
- Post the trainer, never the app. Every clip is about a person. FitRush gets mentioned once, at the end.

## Faster paths

1. **The Oct 1 deadline beats any content strategy for raw speed.** Seven weeks. Send 200 DMs. That is two evenings on the couch.
2. **Referrals from trainers you already signed.** Ask all of them who else at their gym should be on this.
3. **Film before you are ready.** Episode one on a phone, barely edited, this week, beats a polished episode three weeks from now. You learn the format by shooting it.
4. **Fix Me clips can run alone.** On weeks you are slammed, skip the interview entirely and post the eight-second form correction. That clip is the whole product demo.
